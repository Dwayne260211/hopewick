                    🥗 Nutrition

                    Education, not individual medical advice

                    This section is for education, not individual medical advice. Your health, medications and recovery are individual. Some supplements, compounds and major dietary changes can interact with medicines or medical conditions, so speak with an appropriate health professional when that applies to you.

                    Alcohol and benzodiazepine withdrawal can be medically dangerous, including seizures, and can need medical management. That is separate from ordinary nutrition such as meals, water, or breakfast. Call 000 for severe symptoms (confusion, seizures, chest pain, trouble breathing). If someone may have taken too much paracetamol, call 000 or the Poisons Information Centre 13 11 26. A shop supplement is not the response. This page is not crisis support.

                    Recovery can be a period of rebuilding and experimentation. Food, sleep, water, movement, and ordinary routine are easy to neglect while using. Recovery can bring curiosity back: What helps me feel stronger? What helps me sleep? Which foods leave me feeling better? Am I eating enough? Am I drinking enough water? Is this something to discuss with a doctor, pharmacist, or dietitian?

                    • Recovery gave me permission to try things differently.
                    • You do not have to keep living in the same body routines that existed during addiction.
                    • Nutrition is not about finding a magic supplement. It is about becoming curious about what your body needs while it rebuilds.
                    • Try things. Learn. Pay attention to how you respond. Keep what genuinely helps you and be willing to change what does not.

                    From the founder — personal experience, not evidence

                    During my own recovery I became willing to experiment with things I had never considered before, from changing the way I ate to exploring supplements and other approaches. Not everything I tried will be right for everyone, and my experience isn’t medical evidence. What mattered was that I stopped neglecting myself and started becoming curious about what helped me rebuild.

                    🌱 Gut health

                    General information only — not medical advice

                    These notes are general education, not medical advice, not a diagnosis, and not a meal plan written for you. A food or a probiotic is not a treatment for depression, anxiety, or addiction. Talk to a GP or dietitian before you start a probiotic or make a large change to how you eat — especially if you take medicine, are pregnant, or already have a gut condition. In immediate danger call 000. Severe pain, blood in the stool, being unable to keep fluids down, or confusion needs care now. This page is not crisis support.

                    The gut and the brain stay in two-way conversation: nerves, hormones, immune signals, and the microbes that live along the digestive tract. Food, sleep, stress, alcohol, and some other drugs all change that conversation. These notes cover what is established, what is still early, and the ordinary habits worth doing either way.

                    🧠 Brain habits

                    About this information

                    Hopewick provides general education and practical tools and does not replace personalised medical or psychological care. If you are in immediate danger or at risk of harming yourself or someone else, call 000 or use Get help now.

                    Thoughts, feelings, cravings, and habits are things a person has learned to do, often under pressure.                     🧊 Ice baths & recovery spas

                    You spent years training yourself to escape discomfort. Recovery gives you opportunities to practise meeting discomfort differently. An ice bath is one optional way to do that.

                    Before you get in

                    Cold-water immersion places significant stress on the cardiovascular and respiratory systems and is not appropriate for everyone. If you have a heart condition, cardiovascular disease, rhythm problems, an implanted cardiac device, uncontrolled blood pressure, respiratory disease, circulation problems, pregnancy, a significant medical condition, or any concern about whether cold exposure is safe for you, speak with your GP or treating clinician before you attempt it.

                    Never use an ice bath while intoxicated or impaired. Avoid doing it alone. Enter gradually rather than diving in. Stop immediately if you have chest pain, severe shortness of breath, faintness, confusion, unusual heart symptoms, or anything that feels medically wrong, and call 000.

                    Practice notes

                    Optional. The point is a commitment you keep with yourself.

                    Alongside the rest of your recovery

                    This is an optional personal practice. It sits alongside meetings, counselling, medical care, healthy relationships, sleep, exercise, and other established recovery supports.

                    An ice bath is not a treatment for substance dependence, withdrawal, trauma, depression, or any medical condition. It does not detox the body, cure addiction, permanently increase dopamine, or heal the nervous system, and it does not replace professional treatment or the people you trust.

                    If thinking feels unsafe, or you are in danger, use Get help and call 000.
