## Positioning (keep central)
Your service stays in charge of care; Hopewick fills the quiet hours when people are on their own.

Hope is for people in recovery **between sessions** — not a clinical decision-making tool and not a crisis line. Clinicians can still recommend it.

## Who does what
- **Client:** uses Hope on their phone (invite code).
- **Staff:** choose who gets a code, give a short safety brief, decide whether to continue / extend / ongoing licence.
- **Bridge&Bite:** holds the AI key, issues codes, usage caps, email support; can share privacy-preserving pilot summaries (codes activated, general feature use, retention, anonymous satisfaction) — never a conversation feed.

## Simple pilot week
1. Agree 4–6 weeks / 5–10 client codes / one named contact.
2. Bridge&Bite emails codes to your contact.
3. Staff brief: between sessions; not counselling; not for emergencies.
4. Clients use the PWA on their own.
5. Mid-pilot check-in; then continue the service, extend the pilot, or move to an ongoing licence.

## Sector fit
Complements counselling / case work / peer / residential step-down for **adult** AOD services. Private by design — staff do not receive a feed of client conversations. Youth settings need separate safeguarding work before we recommend them.

## Staff time
~15–30 min team brief once; 2–5 min per client code; one short mid-pilot check. No daily staff login.

## What success looks like (without unproven outcome claims)
Another option in hard moments; coping practice between sessions; better prep for appointments; connection to existing recovery activities; positive participant/staff feedback. No claims on relapse / hospitalisation / treatment outcomes until evidenced.
