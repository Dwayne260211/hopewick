Why I built Hopewick

Keeping hope lit between sessions.

Hopewick is not a medical or crisis service. It isn’t treatment, and I’m not a medical professional. If you’re in danger call 000. For crisis support call Lifeline on 13 11 14.

I grew up in foster care. By 17 I was in jail. For years after that, trauma and addiction ran my life.

Rehab is where things started to turn. Around Easter last year, for about 10 days, all the caseworkers were on leave. New people kept getting admitted. The ones detoxing had nothing to do.

The smoke pit was all about using. People reminiscing about the old days. I stayed away from it, because hanging around that put me at risk of relapse.

I was the quietest person in the room. I thought I had to carry everything on my own. A lot of us coming out of addiction are like that. We carry so much guilt, and we worry about what people will think. So we hold back.

I had AI with me. It gave me the courage to walk up to the staff on site and say, “I’m really struggling.”

The foundations of my recovery came from getting the right information for where I was at. How trauma and addiction work together. I’d go straight to what the research says and ask, “What do I need to do to apply this to my life?”

I did gratitudes every day for 9 months. It changed my mindset.

Now I’m doing a TAFE dual diploma in Mental Health and AOD. I graduate in January 2027. I’ve just finished a placement at QuIHN.

That’s where Hopewick comes from. I want to fill the gap for when the workers aren’t there. Somewhere you can journal, reflect and write your gratitudes.

AI doesn’t replace people. It helps you find the words to reach them.

There’s a lot of lonely people out there. I’m tired of seeing empty chairs in NA rooms.

Hopewick is built on harm minimisation and trauma-informed care. It won’t suit everybody. But if it helps one in five people, that could be a life saved.

I’m not a medical professional. Hopewick isn’t treatment and it isn’t a crisis service. It’s just something to help keep hope lit between sessions.

Dwayne Stevens · Founder, Hopewick · TAFE Dual Diploma in Mental Health & Alcohol and Other Drugs (graduating January 2027) · Recently completed a placement at QuIHN. Not a doctor, psychologist or registered counsellor.
